### The Junk Wax Era

#### 1984-85 O-Pee-Chee & Topps
- The beginning of the Junk Wax era
- OPC: 396 bilingual cards (Canada); Topps: 165 English cards (US)
- OPC production estimated at roughly 6 million packs, based on a contest run that year
- Notable rookies: Steve Yzerman, Doug Gilmour, Chris Chelios

#### 1985-86 O-Pee-Chee & Topps
- The Mario Lemieux RC set
- OPC down to 264 bilingual cards; Topps 165 cards plus a sticker in each pack
- Notable OPC rookies: Lemieux, Al MacInnis, Kirk Muller

#### 1986-87 O-Pee-Chee & Topps
- The Patrick Roy RC set
- OPC steady at 264 cards; Topps up slightly to 198 cards plus a sticker
- Notable OPC rookies: Roy, Petr Klima, Wendel Clark

#### 1987-88 O-Pee-Chee & Topps
- OPC stayed at 264 cards; Topps continued with 198 cards and one sticker per pack
- Notable OPC RCs: Adam Oates, Luc Robitaille, Stephane Richer, Esa Tikkanen, Rick Tocchet
- OPC released its first "Minis" set (42 cards)

#### 1988-89 O-Pee-Chee & Topps
- The Brett Hull RC set
- OPC continued at 264 cards; Topps with 198
- OPC Minis returned (46 cards)
- Rack packs returned to mass-market stores for the first time since 1982-83: 42-card sealed cello packs including a single "Mini" card
- Wayne Gretzky traded to Los Angeles over the summer, fueling interest in hockey south of the border

#### 1989-90 The End of the Monopoly
- This was the last year O-Pee-Chee and Topps held exclusive rights to produce hockey cards in Canada and the US
- OPC set increased to 330 bilingual cards; standout RCs are Joe Sakic and Brian Leetch
- Topps set was 198 cards; rumoured to have a lower print run than OPC (unverifiable urban legend)
- OPC dropped the Minis
- Upper Deck releases their first Baseball set 
    - Premium card stock, anti-counterfeit holograms, and foil packs that could not be tampered
    - At $1 a pack (when Topps / Fleer / Donruss were ¢0.45) they sold out in days


#### 1990-91 Upper Deck And The Speculators Arrive
- NHL issues new licenses to Pro Set, Pinnacle, and Upper Deck: the hobby explodes from two sets to nine
- All producers are allowed two hockey sets each
  - Some opt for French / English, some produce "regular" & "premium" sets
- Set sizes explode:
  - Pro Set: enormous 705-card set, with countless errors
  - Upper Deck: 550-card sets (one English, one French) released for the first time across two Series, with "High" Series Factory sets. First Young Guns cards. French set frenzy as the Canada-only edition reportedly had much lower print runs
  - O-Pee-Chee: 528-card "main" set, "limited" 132-card Premier set. Premier sets the hobby world on fire, further stoking speculation. Also available as a Factory set 
  - Topps: 396-card main set, "premium" 264 card Bowman set, still with gum!
  - Score (Pinnacle): two 440 cards sets; one American, one Canadian, also in Factory Sets
- Collecting every base set this year would have totaled 4,005 cards!
- Upper Deck angers collectors with their "High" Series packs, contained a mix of Low and High Series cards  
- Upper Deck and Pro Set go back to the presses for multiple print runs as demand continues  
- Factors driving speculation:
  - Eric Lindros mania
  - First Beckett hockey price guide fuels the speculation market - Wayne Gretzky graces the first cover
  - O-Pee-Chee Premier's rumoured limited print run and frenzy around the Jagr / Roenick / Fedorov RCs
- Counterfeit Patrick Roy and Brett Hull OPC cards begin to surface


#### 1991-92 Junk Wax Era Continues with More Sets
- Peak Junk Wax: 17 total sets, with more inserts and more production
- Releases by company:
  - OPC: one regular set, one Premier set
  - Topps: one regular set, Bowman, and the premium Stadium Club set
  - Upper Deck: one English set, one French set and an English / Czech set of World Junior players
  - Pinnacle: three Score sets (English, Canadian bilingual, American) and two Pinnacle sets (English and French)
  - Pro Set: regular, French and Platinum, plus Parkhurst in English and French
- Score, Bowman, Upper Deck Series 2, and OPC Premier also released factory sets
- First McDonald's Upper Deck "All-Star" set released

#### 1992-93 Junk Wax Era Winds Down, Pro Set out of business
- Sets dialed back to 13; this was the year of peak inserts, led by Upper Deck
- Releases by company:
  - Upper Deck: one French, one English
  - OPC: main and Premier
  - Pinnacle: four sets (two Score, two Pinnacle)
  - Pro Set: only two (main plus Parkhurst)
  - Topps: main and Stadium Club
  - Fleer: new entrant with the premium Ultra set
- Pro Set's planned Series 2 was never released; the company declared bankruptcy the following year, and this was its final hockey set
- Marvel buys Fleer, chasing the card boom
- OPC produced the last-ever set printed in Canada, with a unique design, a reprint insert set, and re-imagined iconic rookie cards from the past

#### 1993-94 Consolidation Begins
- Fourteen total sets
- Donruss debuts, produced by Leaf
- Fleer tried something different with the "tall boy" PowerPlay set alongside Ultra
- Leaf released a standard set
- OPC and Topps each released a Premier set (both printed in the US)
- Topps released another Stadium Club, plus the oddly named Stadium Club O-Pee-Chee set for Canada
- Upper Deck released two sets: a main set and a Parkhurst set, after buying the rights from Pro Set
- Pinnacle and sister brand Score had two sets each

#### 1994-95 Mores Sets, but Smaller Sizes
- Twenty sets hit the market
- Upper Deck expanded to seven sets across the Parkhurst, SP, Be A Player, and Upper Deck brands:
  - Main Upper Deck set and a Be A Player set
  - Parkhurst nostalgia: 1956-57 "Missing Link" set (no Parkie set existed that year), 1964-65 "Tall Boys" set, Parkhurst SE, and a regular Parkhurst set
- Leaf: namesake set, Leaf Limited, and Donruss
- Fleer: Ultra and Flair
- Topps: Premier, Stadium Club, and Finest
- Pinnacle: Select, Score, and a main Pinnacle set
- OPC again had just a single Premier set, produced by Topps
- NHL season shortened by a lockout

#### 1995-96 Shift To Smaller Sets Continues, Focus on "Scarcity"
- Shift to smaller sets and chase cards continued, with a staggering 24 sets
- Topps: five sets, including Bowman (165 cards), Super Skills (90), Finest (191), Stadium Club (225), and main set (385)
- Fleer: Metal (200), SkyBox E-Motion (200), SkyBox Impact (250), and Ultra (400)
- Leaf: main set (330), Limited (120), Donruss (390), and Donruss Elite (110)
- Pinnacle: five sets, including a main set, two Score sets, Select Certified (144 cards, with a Gold parallel), and Zenith (150)
- Upper Deck: main set, Be A Player, Collector's Choice, and two Parkhurst sets, including the 150-card 1966-67 nostalgia "what-if" set (the original Parkhurst company went out of business in 1964)
- Marvel files for bankruptcy, taking the Fleer / SkyBox imprints with it

#### Long Term Impact
- The 2004-05 NHL lockout leads to a complete restructuring of NHL card licensing, with the NHL looking for credibility and stability: Upper Deck is granted the sole license
- Upper Deck officially acquires Fleer / SkyBox and Parkhurst brands in 2005, and the O-Pee-Chee brand in 2006
